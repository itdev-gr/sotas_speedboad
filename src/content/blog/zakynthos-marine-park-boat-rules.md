Laganas Bay offers memorable boating within an important loggerhead turtle habitat. The sound approach is straightforward: proceed cautiously, steer clear of closures and give wildlife generous space. ARCHELON records six nesting beaches along 5.5 kilometres of shoreline, with an annual average of 1,200 nests ([ARCHELON](https://archelon.gr/en/project-areas/zakynthos-laganas-bay), retrieved 2026-07-17).

This overview covers Zakynthos Marine Park boat rules, regulated-zone navigation and low-impact wildlife viewing. For alternative itineraries, see our separate [Turtle Island, Keri Caves and Mizithres guide](/blog/turtle-island-keri-caves-mizithres-rocks-boat-zakynthos).

> **Key Takeaways**
> - Respect every delineated no-go boundary and the local 6-knot limit.
> - Zone A excludes boat movement, while Zone B prohibits mooring.
> - Follow ARCHELON advice on 15-metre distance and 10-minute observation.
> - On-site signs and official directions always take priority.

## What are the quick Zakynthos Marine Park boat rules?

Three-zone controls regulate navigation through exclusions and a 6-knot ceiling. Separately, the endorsed turtle-friendly code from ARCHELON advises a side-on approach, at least 15 metres of separation and a 10-minute encounter maximum ([ARCHELON](https://archelon.gr/en/project-areas/zakynthos-laganas-bay), retrieved 2026-07-17). That voluntary code is protective guidance, not statutory law.

- Keep boats outside marked no-go water, including Zone A.
- In Zones B and C, do not exceed 6 knots.
- Never moor in Zone B. Zone C allows mooring, subject to current signs and instructions.
- Only two vessels should watch one turtle simultaneously.
- Observers should travel below 2 knots; other craft should stay at least 45 metres away.
- Minimise noise. Never feed, touch or swim with a turtle.

These distances are minimum safeguards. When an animal changes course or appears hemmed in, widen the gap and move onward.

## Why does Laganas Bay need a marine park?

Six loggerhead nesting beaches span 5.5 kilometres of Laganas Bay, where ARCHELON reports approximately 1,200 nests in an average year. Established in 1999, the National Marine Park of Zakynthos helps protect this breeding ground and the broader marine ecosystem ([ARCHELON](https://archelon.gr/en/project-areas/zakynthos-laganas-bay), retrieved 2026-07-17).

Navigation restrictions curb disturbance where turtles feed, rest and surface for air. Shoreline protections also support nesting females and emerging hatchlings.

Wild sightings are never guaranteed. A successful encounter leaves natural behaviour unchanged.

## Zakynthos Marine Park boat rules for Zones A, B and C

The framework published by former park managers divides protected water into three zones. Archived NMPZ material excludes boat movement from Zone A. Zone B sets a 6-knot maximum and forbids mooring, whereas Zone C shares that speed cap but allows mooring ([archived NMPZ guidance](https://nmp-zak.org/en/content/management-marine-ecosystems), retrieved 2026-07-17).

### Zone A: no boat movement

Do not enter. Skirt the marked perimeter using updated buoys, signage and your operator briefing.

### Zone B: slow passage, no mooring

Passage is possible wherever present controls permit. Stay at or below 6 knots; do not moor.

### Zone C: slow passage, mooring allowed

The 6-knot maximum applies here too. Historical guidance permits mooring, although posted notices, seabed conditions and official instructions determine acceptable stopping points.

Because the NMPZ website is archival, an old map or screenshot cannot confirm access. Consult fresh markers and pre-departure information instead.

## What does the 6-knot speed limit mean in practice?

Protected-zone controls impose a 6-knot ceiling, while ARCHELON recommends travelling below 2 knots during turtle observation. That lower figure concerns an active wildlife encounter; the general maximum never justifies a close or hurried pass ([ARCHELON](https://archelon.gr/en/project-areas/zakynthos-laganas-bay), retrieved 2026-07-17).

Treat six knots as an upper boundary, not a target. Decelerate well before swimmers, buoys, anchored craft or animals. Poor visibility demands an immediate reduction.

## How should you observe a turtle from a boat?

The endorsed ARCHELON code recommends no more than two observing vessels per turtle. Each should approach from the side, travel below 2 knots, leave 15 metres or more and depart within 10 minutes. Other boats should maintain at least 45 metres ([ARCHELON](https://archelon.gr/en/project-areas/zakynthos-laganas-bay), retrieved 2026-07-17).

A lateral approach preserves open water ahead and astern of the animal. Never cross its route, encircle it or hold it near the surface.

Already see two boats nearby? Remain beyond the 45-metre buffer and carry on instead of joining a queue.

Feeding, touching and swimming with turtles contradict the code. If one surfaces unexpectedly, select neutral when safe, let it clear the vessel and resist chasing a photograph.

## Anchoring and nesting beach access

Archived zone guidance forbids mooring in Zone B but permits it in Zone C. ARCHELON states that nesting beaches may be visited from 07:00 until sunset, prohibits night access and disallows private beach equipment on Marathonisi ([ARCHELON](https://archelon.gr/en/project-areas/zakynthos-laganas-bay), retrieved 2026-07-17).

Confirm any stopping place through same-day briefing and signage. Mooring permission never guarantees beach access.

Once ashore, remain near the seafront, avoid protective cages and remove every item of rubbish. Never handle hatchlings or cross closed sections. Equipment restrictions at Marathonisi help preserve nesting ground.

## What should self-drive renters receive at the briefing?

A useful briefing translates the zone framework into date-specific instructions. Before a [licence-free self-drive rental](/license-free-rent) leaves, renters should understand no-go boundaries, the speed limit, approved stopping places and wildlife-viewing distances ([ARCHELON](https://archelon.gr/en/project-areas/zakynthos-laganas-bay) and [archived NMPZ guidance](https://nmp-zak.org/en/content/management-marine-ecosystems), retrieved 2026-07-17).

Your operator should identify navigable water and relevant buoys. Ask where Zone A starts, which locations prohibit mooring and how to respond if a turtle surfaces nearby.

Previous boating experience never replaces this briefing. Local restrictions can change.

## What if signs or instructions differ from this guide?

The former National Marine Park website identifies itself as an archival resource that is no longer updated. Its notice cites a 2022 decision transferring management to the Greek Natural Environment and Climate Change Agency, NECCA ([archived NMPZ guidance](https://nmp-zak.org/en/content/management-marine-ecosystems), retrieved 2026-07-17). Consequently, those pages provide historical context rather than a live rule feed.

Current signs, buoyed boundaries, same-day briefings, and Coast Guard or park directions override this article. If messages conflict, reduce speed, stay outside the uncertain sector and contact your operator.

## What does a responsible itinerary look like?

The 10-minute ARCHELON recommendation makes turtle viewing a short encounter, not the sole trip objective. After watching from the advised 15-metre distance, leave calmly and explore permitted coastline, caves or swimming spots beyond restricted water ([ARCHELON](https://archelon.gr/en/project-areas/zakynthos-laganas-bay), retrieved 2026-07-17).

Flexibility matters. Bypass closures and crowded sightings promptly. The [Turtle Island and Keri route guide](/keri-caves-turtle-island-boat-tour) describes the wider area; this page addresses conduct inside protected waters.

## Frequently asked questions

The answers below distinguish archived zone restrictions from wildlife-viewing guidance by ARCHELON. Live official instructions may be stricter, and sightings remain uncertain because loggerheads are wild animals in a protected habitat ([ARCHELON](https://archelon.gr/en/project-areas/zakynthos-laganas-bay), retrieved 2026-07-17).

### Can I take a boat into Zone A?

No. Archived park guidance closes Zone A to boat movement. Remain beyond its marked boundary, using updated buoys and official directions if they differ from an older map.

### Can I anchor in Zone B?

No. The archived framework permits navigation in Zone B at no more than 6 knots but prohibits mooring. Its Zone C summary allows mooring, subject to live local controls.

### Can I swim with a loggerhead turtle?

No. The ARCHELON turtle-friendly code advises against swimming with, touching or feeding turtles. Watch quietly from the boat, approach laterally and preserve at least 15 metres of space.

### What happens if more than two boats find one turtle?

Only two vessels should observe simultaneously. Every additional boat should remain 45 metres or more from the turtle. Usually, proceeding creates less pressure than waiting nearby.

### Are nesting beaches open at night?

No. ARCHELON says designated nesting beaches may be visited between 07:00 and sunset, with night access prohibited. Check posted notices because individual sites can carry further restrictions.
