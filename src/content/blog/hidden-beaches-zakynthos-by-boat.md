The most rewarding hidden beaches in Zakynthos by boat are not one fixed itinerary. They are a collection of small pebble shores, narrow coves, natural pools and rock formations spread around very different coasts. Some suit a relaxed local rental. Others lie on exposed stretches where a skipper, a suitable boat and calm water matter far more than a wish list.

This guide focuses on lesser-known swim stops rather than repeating the usual Navagio, Blue Caves or Turtle Island circuit. Use it to build a realistic shortlist, then match that list to your departure port, available time and the operator's permitted route. A [private cruise with a skipper](/rent-with-skipper) is the sensible choice for the more remote west and southwest coast.

## Quick Shortlist: 7 Hidden Beaches in Zakynthos by Boat

These seven locations are distinct, verifiable places. Several are coves or geological features rather than conventional beaches.

1. **Ladi Beach**, a small boat-access shore on the west coast
2. **Sfogio Beach**, a boat-access shore on the northwest coast
3. **Filippoi Beach and cove**, a rocky inlet below the Volimes area
4. **Porto Steniti**, a remote west-coast cove near Maries
5. **Korakonisi**, a rock arch and enclosed swimming cove
6. **Pisines**, a southwest-coast natural-pool swim stop
7. **Eros Cave area**, a weather-dependent cave and rock formation

“Hidden” does not mean empty or untouched. Small bays receive rentals and tours too. Their appeal is their scale, scenery and small-boat access, not guaranteed solitude.

## What does “boat access” mean here?

Boat access may be the only practical route, an easier alternative to a steep path, or the best viewpoint for a formation. It never guarantees a safe or permitted landing. Weather, route boundaries and the operator's instructions still apply.

Sfogio and Ladi are boat-only or overwhelmingly boat-primary. Filippoi, Porto Steniti and Korakonisi have land approaches that may be narrow, steep or rough. Pisines and Eros Cave are boat-based swim or sightseeing stops, not promised landings.

Navagio offers a useful contrast. Landing and swimming remained prohibited for the 2026 season at the time of writing, while vessel approach was controlled. Rules can change, so follow current Port Authority and operator instructions. Our [Navagio and Blue Caves guide](/blog/navagio-shipwreck-beach-blue-caves-boat-zakynthos) provides context, but current restrictions take priority.

## 1. Ladi Beach: a small west-coast shore

**Coast:** [Ladi Beach](https://isalaadmin.safecontrol.gr/storage/attachments/cus_isalaadmin/Page/9/zakynthos_guide_isala_travel_2023.pdf) is a small west-coast shore reached on local Porto Vromi coastal routes. Pale cliffs and clear water create a compact swim stop with no dependable facilities.

**Why go:** It offers a quieter-scale alternative to the headline west-coast landmark, with the boat itself providing the practical access.

**Access note:** Swimming and anchoring may be possible in suitable conditions, but boats should keep clear of the cliffs because small rockfalls can occur. From Zakynthos Marina, use a skipper unless the operator explicitly confirms the self-drive boundary.

**Best fit:** Calm-weather west-coast trips with a skipper.

## 2. Sfogio Beach: a genuine boat-access shore

**Coast:** [Sfogio Beach](https://www.greeka.com/ionian/zakynthos/beaches/sfogio/) lies on the rugged northwest coast near Anafonitria. It has a coarse sand-and-pebble shore, deep water, high rock walls and no facilities.

**Why go:** The beach appears below the cliffs without a road or organised seafront, creating a memorable small-boat arrival.

**Access note:** It is reached by boat, but landing depends on swell, depth and skipper judgement. Rental route permission varies by base.

**Best fit:** A skippered northwest cruise, or explicitly permitted self-drive route.

## 3. Filippoi Beach and cove: rocks, clear water and a steep backdrop

**Coast:** [Filippoi Beach](https://www.greeka.com/ionian/zakynthos/beaches/filippoi/) is a rocky northwest cove below Volimes. Large stones line its shore, a cave opening sits to one side and the road becomes very steep.

**Why go:** A boat avoids the difficult descent and opens a clear view towards the cove's layered rock walls.

**Access note:** Waves can complicate water entry near the rocks. Cave entry is never guaranteed, and the stop may remain offshore.

**Best fit:** Confident swimmers and small, skippered northwest groups.

## 4. Porto Steniti: a long west-coast cove near Maries

**Coast:** [Ormos Stenitis](https://anchorages.co/go/greece/ormos-stenitis/), also called Porto Steniti or Porto Stenitis, lies below Maries on western Zakynthos. It is a rocky limestone cove rather than a classic beach, with no dependable waterside facilities.

**Why go:** Its narrow shape creates changing cliff views and, on calm days, an atmospheric swim away from the open coast.

**Access note:** A land route exists, but boat access remains conditional. Surge or reflected wash can enter the cove, so the skipper chooses the stop.

**Best fit:** Private west-coast trips focused on swimming and scenery.

## 5. Korakonisi: a rock arch and swimming cove, not a beach

**Coast:** [Korakonisi](https://www.allovergreece.com/Natural-Monument/Descr/26/en) is a west-coast limestone formation with a natural arch beside an enclosed cove. A land path and stone steps also reach it.

**Why go:** From the water, the arch frames rock ledges, shadow and open sea, creating a dramatic alternative to a normal beach.

**Access note:** Western wind and swell can roughen the entrance or prevent anchoring. A skipper may substitute a sheltered cove.

**Best fit:** Strong swimmers on a calm-weather skippered cruise.

## 6. Pisines: natural pools for a southwest swim

**Coast:** [Pisines](https://sailgreeceguide.com/ionian/zakynthos/), meaning “pools”, names a clear-water cove and swim area on the remote southwest coast. It is neither a managed pool nor a serviced beach.

**Why go:** It offers a boat-based swim among pale limestone, where water colour shifts with sunlight, cloud and sea movement.

**Access note:** Current excursions list Pisines as weather-dependent. Treat the exposed run as an optional extension, not a guaranteed stop.

**Best fit:** A southwest skipper cruise. See [Turtle Island, Keri Caves and Mizithres by boat](/blog/turtle-island-keri-caves-mizithres-rocks-boat-zakynthos) for the neighbouring route.

## 7. Eros Cave area: a remote cave stop for settled conditions

**Coast:** [Eros Cave](https://welcometozante.com/en/holiday-to-zante/excursion-eros-cave-pisines-mizithres/) shares the remote southwest sector with Pisines. It is a local tourism name for a sea-cave and rock-passage area, not a conventional beach.

**Why go:** Its tighter limestone setting, light and entrance shape contrast with open swim bays, even when viewed from outside.

**Access note:** For Sota departures, treat this as a skippered route unless written self-drive permission is provided. Swell, visibility, boat type or swimmer ability can cancel cave approach or entry. Enter only with explicit approval.

**Best fit:** Competent swimmers on a weather-dependent private southwest cruise.

## Should you choose self-drive or a skipper?

Choose self-drive only inside the operating area defined during your briefing. A no-licence rental does not open the whole island, and departure points serve different sectors. A west-coast stop may sit inside one operator's boundary and outside another's.

A skipper is better for Porto Steniti, Korakonisi, Pisines and Eros Cave, and often Sfogio or Filippoi. Their value is not guaranteed access. It is making qualified route changes when exposed or remote stops become unsuitable.

## How should you choose the route and duration?

Build the day around one cluster. Ladi, Sfogio, Filippoi and Porto Steniti suit a northwest or west plan. Korakonisi, Pisines and Eros Cave require separate west or southwest assessment.

A four-hour booking may suit a nearby cluster and two or three swims when the departure port is already close. Seven hours can allow a wider coastal section and more flexibility, but neither duration guarantees a remote stop from every harbour. Confirm the realistic range for the chosen base and boat.

## Safety, anchoring and landing caveats

Every stop is conditional. Wind, swell, visibility, traffic and local notices can alter the route. Operator boundaries may also be narrower than a boat's technical range.

Do not expect to land because a place is called a beach. Stones, submerged rock, shore break and cliffs may make an offshore swim safer. Anchoring depends on depth, seabed, traffic, swimmers and restrictions.

Keep away from the propeller, wait for the skipper's signal and use the ladder correctly. Do not climb cliffs or jump from ledges. At caves and regulated areas, current authority and operator instructions override every saved itinerary.

## What should you bring?

Pack for a boat-based swim day with little or no access to shops:

- High-factor, water-resistant sunscreen, a hat and sunglasses
- Drinking water and food suitable for the booked duration
- Swimwear, towel and a dry layer for the return journey
- Water shoes for pebbles, ladders and rocky edges
- A well-fitting snorkel mask if you plan to explore from the surface
- A waterproof phone pouch, used without taking risks near rocks
- Any personal medication, plus motion-sickness support if advised

## Frequently asked questions

### Can you visit all seven places in one boat trip?

Not sensibly on a normal half-day route. The seven stops span the northwest, west and southwest coasts. Choose one coastal cluster and leave time for swimming. A full-day skipper may cover more, but weather, departure port and route permissions decide what is realistic.

### Are these hidden beaches suitable for a self-drive boat?

Some may be, but only when your rental company includes them inside its operating area and the day's conditions are suitable. Remote west and southwest locations are generally better with a skipper. Never plan from an online map alone, because permitted boundaries vary by operator and departure point.

### Will the boat land at each beach?

No. Landing is never promised. Several places in this list are coves, natural pools or cave areas rather than beaches. Even at Sfogio or Ladi, waves, rocks, depth and local restrictions may require an offshore swim stop, a slow pass or a substitute bay.

### Which stops are best for families?

These remote stops are not the default family route. Ladi or Sfogio may work in calm conditions when a skipper approves the stop, while Filippoi, Korakonisi, Pisines and Eros Cave require more careful assessment because of rocks, depth or exposure. Tell the operator about children, non-swimmers and mobility needs before selecting the route.
